A question tag is a short question added at the end of a statement to confirm, check, or seek agreement. This complete reference covers every rule that appears in the SEE English paper, with examples, worked solutions, and 50 questions from past exams.

Class 10 English Grammar | Reviewed for SEE 2083 Syllabus

01 Definition

A question tag is a short question added at the end of a statement to confirm, check, or seek agreement.

Examples

  • You are coming, aren't you?
  • She doesn't like tea, does she?

The Golden Rule

Positive Statement → Negative Tag Negative Statement → Positive Tag
Statement TypeTag TypeExample
PositiveNegativeShe is here, isn't she?
NegativePositiveShe isn't here, is she?

02 The Master Formula

03 Rules of Question Tags

Rule 1 Auxiliary Verbs (is, am, are, was, were)
StatementTag
She is a doctor,isn't she?
They are playing,aren't they?
I am late,aren't I?
He was present,wasn't he?
We were wrong,weren't we?
It is cold,isn't it?
You are tired,aren't you?
Special Case: "I am" → "aren't I?" (never "amn't I?")
Rule 2 Present Simple (do/does)
StatementTag
You like tea,don't you?
She plays tennis,doesn't she?
He works hard,doesn't he?
They live here,don't they?
Ram goes to school,doesn't he?
We need help,don't we?
It rains a lot,doesn't it?
Rule: I/you/we/they/plural → don't  |  He/she/it/singular → doesn't
Rule 3 Past Simple (did)
StatementTag
You went there,didn't you?
She cooked food,didn't she?
They played well,didn't they?
He came late,didn't he?
I saw him,didn't I?
Rule: Past simple → always didn't (for all subjects)
Rule 4 Present Perfect (has/have)
StatementTag

She has gone,hasn't she?
They have arrived,haven't they?
He has eaten,hasn't he?
Rule 5 Past Perfect (had)
StatementTag

She had eaten,hadn't she?
They had gone,hadn't they?
Rule 6 Modals
StatementTag
You can swim,can't you?
She could help,couldn't she?
He will come,won't he?

We shall go,shan't we?

Rule 7 Imperatives
StatementTag
Close the door,will you?
Don't go out,will you?
Please help me,will you?
Let's go,shall we?
Let me help you,may I?
Let him go,will you?
Have a seat,won't you?
Rules:
• Let's → always shall we?
• Let me → may I?
• Let him/her/them → will you?
• Other imperatives → will you?
• Invitations → won't you?

Rule 9 'Have' — Possession vs. Action
StatementTagReason
You have a car,don't you?Possession → don't
You have finished,haven't you?Action (perfect) → haven't

Rule: If "have" means own/possess → use do/does/did. If "have" is auxiliary (perfect tense) → use have/has/had.

Rule 12 'Neither of' → 'Are They?'

Indefinite pronouns like neither, either, someone, nobody, everyone always shift to the plural pronoun they in a question tag. Because the tag uses they, the auxiliary verb must agree with it and become plural (are).

Exam-Correct Standard:

Neither of the answers is correct, are they?
StatementTag
Neither of the answers is correct,are they?
Neither of them came,did they?
Either of the boys can go,can't they?
Everyone is here,aren't they?
Someone called,didn't they?
Nobody came,did they?
None of them helped,did they?
Exception: For things (not people), use it:
• Nothing happened, did it?
• Everything is fine, isn't it?

StatementTag

Rule 16 Imperatives → 'Will You?'
StatementTag

Have a seat,won't you?
Note: Invitations (Have a cup of tea) → won't you? (polite)
Rule 17 'You'd Better' → 'Hadn't You?'
StatementTag

Rule 18 'You'd Like' → 'Wouldn't You?'
StatementTag

Rule 19 'Ought To' → 'Oughtn't'
StatementTag

They ought to leave,oughtn't they?
Rule 20 'Needn't' → 'Need'
StatementTag
You needn't worry,need you?
She needn't come,need she?
They needn't wait,need they?

08 Decision Flowchart

Is there an auxiliary verb? ↓ YES → Use that auxiliary in opposite form ↓ NO → Use do/does/did ↓ Is the subject I? ↓ YES → Use aren't I? ↓ NO → Use regular pronoun ↓ Is the statement negative? ↓ YES → Use positive tag ↓ NO → Use negative tag ↓ Is it Let's? ↓ YES → shall we? ↓ Is it an imperative? ↓ YES → will you? (or won't you? for invitation)
